# AI side, Fall weeks 6 and 7: person re-identification and tracking on the laptop

Date: 2026-11-15 (start of week 8). Author: Devin, with Claude Code.
Covers the laptop half of two proposal items:

- Week 6: test ST's person-matching (re-identification) model on a laptop with a free multi-camera dataset.
- Week 7: tracking, with frames per second and accuracy at 1, 3 and 5 people.

The board half of both weeks (camera and detection on the Nucleo, ST's tracking demo on the board) is
not in here: this laptop has no board attached and ST Edge AI Core is not installed on it yet. The
team's `claude/safe-swarm-senior-design-i9elpx` branch now holds an STM32 unit skeleton
(`firmware/stm32/App/`, Nov 13) whose `tracker.c`, `detect.c` and `behavior.c` are marked as Devin's;
the laptop tracker below is a line-for-line mirror of that `tracker.c`, so the parameter choices
measured here drop straight into `app_config.h`.

Code: `ai/herd_ai/{reid,reid_metrics,tracker,mot}.py`, `ai/scripts/evaluate_reid.py`,
`ai/scripts/track_sequence.py`, tests in `ai/tests/`. Results: `ai/results/reid/` and `ai/results/tracking/`.

## Decisions

1. **Re-ID test set: Market-1501.** Free, 1,501 people seen by 6 cameras, 3,368 query crops against
   19,732 gallery crops, and the standard protocol (same-camera matches removed, rank-k and mAP) that
   every published re-ID number uses. ST evaluated its models on DeepSportradar (basketball players);
   our demo is pedestrians in ordinary clothes, which is what Market-1501 contains.
2. **Preprocessing copies ST's training config** (`osnet_a025_256_128_tfs_config.yaml`): RGB, stretch
   ("fit") to 256x128, nearest-neighbour resizing, uint8 in. Bilinear resizing was tried first; on a
   200-query / 2,000-gallery subset it made no real difference (mAP 0.055 vs 0.061), so nearest stays.
3. **Matching rule for the board:** dequantize the int8 output with the model's scale and zero point,
   L2-normalize, compare by cosine distance, decide "same person" with a fixed distance threshold. The
   threshold is read off the measured cross-camera same-person / different-person distributions at a
   chosen false-accept rate (1 % and 5 % reported), not guessed.
4. **Beyond rank-1, measure the question the advisor asked on Nov 2:** two (or three, or five) people
   walk into the next camera together and one of them is the person we were told about. For each query
   we draw one true cross-camera image of that person plus k - 1 other people and check whether the
   right one is nearest. That is the number that decides whether appearance data in the handoff
   message is worth its bytes.
5. **Tracker: the firmware's algorithm, in Python, scored on public video.** Nearest center within
   TRACK_MATCH_DIST (200/1000 of the frame), new id otherwise, forget after TRACK_DROP_MS (1 s), at
   most 8 people. Two candidate improvements are implemented as switches and measured rather than
   argued about: matching against the position predicted from the last speed, and IoU (box overlap)
   matching instead of center distance.
6. **Tracking test set: MOT15 static-camera sequences** with the official labels: PETS09-S2L1 (768x576,
   7 fps, 795 frames, up to 8 people: the closest thing to a fixed security camera), TUD-Stadtmitte and
   TUD-Campus (640x480, 25 fps, street level), ADL-Rundle-6 (sidewalk, 30 fps). The videos served by
   motchallenge.net were checked frame-for-frame against the label files (795 / 179 / 71 / 525).
   Metrics are the usual CLEAR MOT ones (recall, precision, id switches, MOTA, mostly-tracked), reported
   overall and split by how many people were in the frame (1-2, 3-4, 5+). The detector alone is scored
   too, so detector misses and tracker faults can be told apart.

## Results

### Week 6: re-identification on Market-1501 (laptop, 2026-11-15)

**Subset run:** 200 query crops against 2,000 gallery crops (fixed seed), 154 queries with a
cross-camera match. The full run (3,368 against 19,732) was started and then stopped by the machine
running out of memory for unrelated reasons; `python scripts/evaluate_reid.py --models "models/osnet_*.tflite"`
reruns it in about 30 minutes and rewrites `ai/results/reid/`. Rank numbers on the subset are
optimistic (fewer distractors in the gallery); the pairwise numbers barely depend on gallery size.

| model | queries / gallery | mAP | rank-1 | rank-5 | ST's own test (DeepSportradar) mAP / rank-1 | median distance, same person other camera vs different person | threshold at 1 % false accepts (true pairs it accepts) | right person nearest out of 2 / 3 / 5 arrivals | laptop embed, median | ST N6570-DK |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| OSNet 0.25 int8 (128-d) | 200 / 2,000 | 0.061 | 0.078 | 0.149 | 0.703 / 0.920 | 0.329 vs 0.446 | 0.134 (8 %) | 75 % / 62 % / 46 % | 9 to 12 ms | 7.2 ms |

Sanity check on 120 queries with both models (medians of cosine distance): same person, same camera
0.084 (OSNet 1.0: 0.115); same person, other camera 0.263 (0.370); different person 0.423 (0.534).

What this says:

- The pipeline is right (near-duplicate crops from the same camera sit at 0.08, far below everything
  else), but the model does not generalize from basketball players to pedestrians: rank-1 of 8 % and mAP
  of 6 % against ST's 92 % / 70 % on its own test set. Published OSNet models trained on pedestrian data
  reach rank-1 above 90 % on this exact benchmark, so the gap is the training data, not the architecture.
- The two distributions overlap heavily. A threshold that keeps false "same person" decisions at 1 %
  accepts only 8 % of true matches; at 5 % false accepts, 21 %. There is no threshold a unit can use on
  its own to say "this is the person I was told about".
- As a tie-breaker it is weak but not useless: when two people arrive together it picks the right one
  75 % of the time (chance is 50 %); with three, 62 % (chance 33 %); with five, 46 % (chance 20 %).
- OSNet 1.0 separates the pairs by about the same margin (0.16) at ten times the cost, so size is not the fix.

What this says:

- On a fixed camera at 25 fps the firmware's tracker loses nothing: recall and precision with ids equal
  the detector's, 8 of 10 people are tracked through more than 80 % of their frames, and an id change
  happens about once every 15 to 20 frames with up to 8 people in view.
- Crowding and low frame rate are what cause id switches. On PETS at 7 fps, frames with 3-4 people see
  one switch per 18 frames, frames with 5 or more see one per 7. People move far between frames at
  7 fps, so a nearest-center match has more candidates within reach.
- Where accuracy is low, it is the detector, not the tracker: on the ADL sidewalk sequence the 256 model
  finds only 48 % of the labeled people (far, small, overlapping) and the 320 model 51 %, the same
  small-person limit measured on COCO in week 5. The tracker cannot recover what was never detected.
- Of the two candidate improvements, neither earns its place. Speed prediction is neutral or worse (a
  one-frame velocity estimate is noisy); IoU matching helps only when boxes are large and overlapping
  (ADL) and hurts when they are small and far apart between frames (PETS). The firmware's nearest-center
  rule stays.
- TRACK_MATCH_DIST 100 beats 200 on the two crowded sequences and ties elsewhere; TRACK_DROP_MS 1000 is
  the best of 500 / 1000 / 2000 everywhere. The 320 detector finds more people (PETS recall 0.84 to
  0.88) at the cost of precision and 6 ms on the board; same trade-off as week 5.

## Findings that affect the plan

- **Appearance alone will not carry the handoff with ST's ready-made re-ID model.** See the numbers
  above. The handoff therefore stays position-and-timing first (which side the person left, when, how
  fast), with the appearance embedding as a tie-breaker when more than one person arrives. Two ways to
  make appearance stronger, both Winter-sized: fine-tune OSNet on Market-1501 through the model zoo
  services (ST documents transfer learning for this use case), or try ST's MobileNetV2 re-ID variant.
- **Embedding size vs the message format.** OSNet 0.25 produces 128 int8 values = 128 bytes (OSNet 1.0:
  512). `firmware/common/herd_msg.h` caps the payload at 32 bytes, so the "heading your way" message
  cannot carry an embedding yet. An ESP-NOW frame carries up to 250 bytes, so raising
  HERD_MSG_MAX_PAYLOAD to about 140 is enough for the 0.25 embedding plus the heading fields in one
  frame; the proposal's 1,280-byte figure (ST's example) is ten times what OSNet 0.25 needs.
- **The tracker is free; the detector is the clock.** The tracker takes under 0.1 ms per frame on the
  laptop against 7 to 12 ms for the detector, and the detector's cost does not depend on how many people
  are in view. "Frames per second with 1, 3 and 5 people" on the N6 will therefore be the same number
  (ST's 23.8 ms for YOLOv8n 256 plus the camera pipeline); what changes with crowding is accuracy,
  which the per-crowd-level columns above show.
- **Tracker settings for app_config.h.** Keep TRACK_DROP_MS at 1000 and TRACK_MAX at 8 (PETS peaks
  at 8 people). Try TRACK_MATCH_DIST 100 to 150 on the board: it cut id switches by 14 % on PETS and
  9 % on ADL with no loss of recall. Keep 200 while the camera is panning, when apparent motion is
  larger; the number should become a test parameter, not a constant. Do not add speed prediction or
  IoU matching to the firmware.
- **Frame rate matters more than the tracker.** The same tracker made 2.2x more id switches per frame
  at 7 fps (PETS) than at 25 fps (TUD) with similar crowd sizes. ST quotes 23.8 ms for the detector on
  the N6, so the board should run near 25 fps if the camera pipeline keeps up; if the real rate comes
  out near 10 fps, expect PETS-like behaviour and lower TRACK_MATCH_DIST accordingly. Measure the
  achieved frame rate first thing on the board.
- **The camera that arrived is a third-party module.** The teammate branch records it as an InnoMaker
  CAM-IMX335-5MP, not ST's B-CAMS-IMX. Same Sony IMX335 sensor, but the 22-pin pinout must be checked
  against UM3300 before plugging it in, and ST's IMX335 driver may need register changes. This is the
  first thing to confirm before any board-side detection work.
- **ST's published OSNet numbers were wrong in the Nov 2 write-up and are corrected there:** with ST
  Edge AI Core 4.0.0 the N6570-DK takes 7.22 ms and 928 KiB internal RAM for OSNet 0.25 and 71.29 ms
  with 2.6 MB internal + 2 MB external RAM for OSNet 1.0. Only the 0.25 model is a realistic fit next
  to the detector; it is also the better one on Market-1501 per byte of RAM.
- **Laptop tooling:** Windows Smart App Control intermittently blocks the unsigned TFLite runtime DLL
  ("An Application Control policy has blocked this file"). A re-run has worked every time so far. If it
  becomes persistent the options are to turn Smart App Control off (cannot be re-enabled without a
  reinstall) or switch runtimes.
