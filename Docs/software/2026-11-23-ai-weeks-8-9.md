Date: 2026-11-23 (start of week 10). Author: Devin, with Claude Code.

## What these two weeks were for

From the proposal schedule: week 8 is "flag someone lingering in one area", week 9 is "first pass at
following the person with the servos". The unit skeleton merged on November 22 (PR #9: `tracker.c`,
`behavior.c`, `pantilt.c`, `unit_fsm.c`, `link.c`, the shared message format, a laptop test harness
and the pin map) already contains a lingering rule and a servo follow law, written and tested
against one made-up person. The work here puts both through real pedestrian video, keeps what
works, changes what does not, and proves that the laptop measurements apply to the board's C.

The board half of weeks 6 and 7 is still blocked: ST Edge AI Core's installer logs in to
`my.st.com`, which stalls for every non-browser client on UCI-WiFi (see "Toolchain" below).

## Decisions

- **Mirror the firmware exactly, then prove it.** `behavior.c` and `pantilt.c` were rebuilt in
  Python (`ai/herd_ai/behavior.py`, `pantilt.py`) with C integer arithmetic (truncating division),
  and the week 7 tracker mirror now keeps the firmware's slot order. A C replay tool
  (`firmware/tests/trace_main.c`, built from the real `tracker.c` + `behavior.c`) and
  `ai/scripts/check_firmware_parity.py` feed the same detection stream to both sides and compare
  every frame's ids, positions, speeds and lingering reports.
- **Score lingering against the rule itself on perfect tracks.** MOT15 has no "loitering" labels.
  A labeled stay is the firmware's rule applied to the labeled tracks with their perfect ids; the
  pipeline (detector, tracker, rule) is scored by whether its reports point at someone who really
  had been standing that long, with every false alarm sorted into "nobody there", "a group was
  there" or "too early", and every miss into "id restarted", "never detected" or "detected too briefly".
- **Simulate the servos with a virtual pan camera over the labeled clips** (`ai/herd_ai/followsim.py`):
  the camera sees half the frame width at 60 degrees, pans until the view hits the frame edge,
  runs the firmware tracker on what it sees, applies each box to the follow law 30 ms after its
  frame and steps the servos every 5 ms like `app_loop()`. One episode per labeled person. The error
  that counts is where the *labeled* person is in the view, so an id switch onto someone else
  counts against the law. Labeled boxes stand in for the detector during the sweep; the real
  detector is run on the camera's crop at the chosen settings.
- **Public data only, plus the webcam for team members.** No clip of a team member exists yet;
  the demo page's webcam stream now shows the lingering rule live (stand still for eight seconds).

## Findings

1. **Following works as written.** Against a fixed camera, the firmware's law keeps the person
   centered two to four times as often and loses far fewer people before the pan limit. Nothing
   in `pantilt.c` needed a different structure.
2. **High gain overshoots at low frame rates.** Above 30 % the camera over-corrects between 7 fps
   frames and swings past people. 15 to 30 % are equivalent; `FOLLOW_GAIN_PCT` stays 30.
   `SERVO_MAX_SPEED_DPS` 120 is never the limit; 60 is enough.
3. **A deadband is free.** Ignoring aiming errors under 40/1000 keeps the centering and cuts
   direction reversals by 15 to 60 %. Added as `FOLLOW_DEADBAND` (40) in `app_config.h`, used in
   `pantilt_follow()`, with a laptop test.
4. **The detector, not the law, limits following on far people.** With the real detector on the
   camera's view, PETS drops from 65 % to 54 % centered and 3 to 12 lost: the small, far people
   this clip is full of are the week 5 limit of the 256 model again.
5. **Lingering fires on the right spots but is held back by id swaps.** Of the six missed stays,
   five had the person detected the whole time; the greedy nearest-center tracker swapped ids
   between neighbours standing in a group and the clock restarted. Eight of the ten false alarms
   were a group occupying the spot, which for a security camera is a reasonable alarm. Two were a
   static non-person detection in the top right of the frame.
6. **Neither guard pays.** Requiring the person to have walked in halves the hits and does not
   remove the static-object alarms (those tracks did move, by jumping onto the object from a
   passer-by); a detection-score floor removes one ghost at the cost of a hit. Both are in the
   firmware behind switches, off (`LINGER_REQUIRE_MOTION 0`, `LINGER_MIN_SCORE 0`), so they can
   be tried on the board.
7. **Keep radius 120 and 8 s.** A smaller radius misses more (box jitter breaks the stay); a
   larger one or a shorter time finds more stays but adds as many alarms. Raising `TRACK_DROP_MS`
   to 2000 trades one miss for two "too early" alarms; week 7 already found it worse for id switches.

## Changes to the unit firmware (all laptop-tested, 415 checks, 0 failed)

- `app_config.h`: `FOLLOW_DEADBAND 40`; `LINGER_REQUIRE_MOTION` and `LINGER_MIN_SCORE` switches
  (both 0); the measured settings are commented with where the numbers come from.
- `pantilt.c`: the deadband in `pantilt_follow()`.
- `tracker.h/.c`: `track_t.score`, the confidence of the last matched box.
- `behavior.c`: the two guards and the `moved` bookkeeping; same output as before with both off.
- `detect.h/.c`: `detect_boxes_to_frame()` packs ST's post-processed boxes (center, size and
  confidence as fractions) into a `frame_t`, most confident first, at most eight, clamped;
  `detect_submit()` hands a real frame to `detect_poll()` when `USE_FAKE_DETECTIONS` is 0. The
  rest of the app is untouched.
- `firmware/tests`: deadband and detection-glue tests in `test_main.c`; `trace_main.c` and a
  `trace_tool` Makefile target; `.gitignore` for the Windows binaries.

## The V1 schematic, from the software side

The hardware team's draft (November 16) keeps what the firmware depends on: the same part family
(STM32N657B0H3Q), the same MX25UM51245G OctoSPI flash as the Nucleo, so the boot layout and the
`MX25UM51245G_STM32N6570-NUCLEO` external loader carry over; USB-C on OTG1 high speed for the
webcam-style video output; and the camera on a 15-pin MIPI CSI-2 ribbon connector with two data
lanes, clock, I2C and a GPIO. That connector is the Raspberry-Pi style, not the Nucleo's 22-pin,
so V1 needs a camera module with that pinout, and ST's camera middleware only needs the sensor to
be one it knows (IMX335 is). Two things to confirm with the hardware team: that USART3 (PD8/PD9)
and TIM1 channels 1 and 2 (PE9/PE11) stay routed to the ESP32 and servo headers as in
`Docs/pin-map.md`, and which UART the debug log uses without an ST-LINK.

## Toolchain notes (this laptop)

- **ST Edge AI Core**: the online installer (4.1.0) stalls at the myST login on UCI-WiFi. Three
  HTTP clients complete the TLS handshake to `my.st.com` and `www.st.com` and then receive nothing,
  while the same edge server answers for other ST hosts; browsers get through. Run the installer at
  home or on a hotspot (`C:\ST\installer`); `ai/scripts/compile_n6.py` is ready for it.
- **STM32CubeIDE 2.1.1** is installed and bundles arm-none-eabi-gcc 14.3, make, the ST-LINK
  gdbserver and STM32CubeProgrammer 2.22.0 with the N6 loaders and signing tool. Use that copy of
  CubeProgrammer: the standalone one is blocked by Windows Smart App Control (unsigned `zlib1.dll`).
- **Firmware laptop tests on Windows**: MSYS2's gcc (`C:\msys64\ucrt64\bin`) builds them; the
  teammate's Makefile works from the MSYS2 UCRT64 shell, and from PowerShell the one-line gcc
  command in `firmware/stm32/README.md` does the same.
- **Firmware package**: `x-cube-n6-ai-people-detection-tracking` v2.2.1 is public on GitHub
  (Nucleo build streams detections over USB as a webcam; NPU runtime generated with Edge AI Core
  3.0.0, so a 4.x compile needs ST's runtime update step). Not cloned yet.
